# Offices Primer: What Each Office Can Influence

Used to decide which of the voter's issues are relevant to a given race. A school board candidate's view on foreign policy tells the voter little; a county sheriff's view on school funding is mostly noise. Rating only the issues an office can move keeps the guide honest.

This is a **U.S.-focused** default. Powers vary by state and locality; check the jurisdiction's own description of the office when it matters, and mark a power "limited" rather than "none" when you are unsure. For other countries, build the same kind of table from the national constitution or electoral authority and keep it in the guide's method notes.

Use these as guidance. The voter's issue categories (see the interview playbook) map as follows.

## Federal

| Office | Can meaningfully influence | Limited or none |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. House of Representatives | Federal budget and taxes, healthcare law, immigration law, national security and foreign policy oversight, trade, energy and environmental law, federal housing and education programs and funding, federal criminal law, oversight of the executive, election law (federal), tech and AI regulation, free speech and religious-liberty law | Local zoning, school curriculum, local policing, most day-to-day public safety |
| U.S. Senate | The same as the House, plus treaty ratification and confirmation of judges and executive officials | Local services |
| President (when on the ballot) | Executive enforcement priorities, foreign policy, appointments, vetoes, federal agencies | Detailed legislation (needs Congress) |

## State

| Office | Can meaningfully influence | Limited or none |
|---|---|---|
| Governor | State budget, signing or vetoing bills, appointments, state agencies, disaster response, executive orders, state policing and corrections policy | Federal immigration or foreign policy |
| Lieutenant governor | Varies by state: presides over senate, succession, sometimes boards and committees | Most executive power |
| Attorney general | Enforcement of state law, consumer protection, civil rights enforcement, litigation for the state, opinions on law, sometimes election and antitrust enforcement | Writing legislation |
| Secretary of state | Elections administration, business filings, records, sometimes licensing | Most policy areas |
| Treasurer, comptroller, auditor | State finances, investments, audits, oversight of spending | Regulatory and social policy |
| Superintendent of public instruction / education commissioner | K-12 policy, standards, state education funding distribution, accountability | Higher-ed tuition (usually separate), local day-to-day operations |
| Agriculture, insurance, labor, public utility/service commissioners | Regulation of their sectors, rates, licensing, consumer protection | Unrelated areas |
| State legislators (senate, house) | State laws and budget, taxes, education funding, healthcare programs, criminal law, housing and zoning rules, local government powers, redistricting (in many states), election law | Federal law |
| State supreme and appellate judges | Interpretation of state constitution and law, review of legislation and executive actions | Making policy directly |

## County and local

| Office | Can meaningfully influence | Limited or none |
|---|---|---|
| County board/commission/supervisor | County budget and taxes, unincorporated-area zoning, county health and social services, roads, jails, elections administration support | School curriculum, state or federal law |
| Sheriff | Law enforcement and jail operations in the county, civil process, sometimes court security and immigration cooperation policy | Writing laws, zoning |
| District attorney / county attorney | Charging decisions, plea policy, prosecution priorities, diversion programs | Police operations, sentencing law |
| Assessor, recorder, treasurer, clerk | Property valuation, land records, tax collection, elections and records administration, licensing | Policy making |
| Coroner / medical examiner | Death investigation | - |
| Mayor | City budget, city agencies, police and fire direction, local development priorities, appointments | State or federal law |
| City or town council | Zoning and land use, local taxes and fees, local ordinances, city budget, local services and infrastructure, housing policy within state law | Federal and state policy |
| School board | District budget, hiring and superintendent, curriculum adoption within state standards, school policies, facilities, bond measures | Statewide funding formulas, federal immigration |
| Special districts (water, fire, library, hospital, community college) | Their narrow service area, budget, and rates | Everything else |
| Local judges | Rulings in individual cases, courtroom practice | Policy making |

## Issue relevance cheat sheet

Quick lookup from the interview issue list (alphabetical) to offices that usually matter most. If an issue is not listed, reason from the office table above and say that the mapping is your judgment.

- **Abortion and reproductive health policy**: Congress, state legislature, governor, attorney general, state courts.
- **Economy and cost of living**: Congress, governor, state legislature, mayor, city council, county board.
- **Education and public schools**: State legislature, governor, state superintendent, school board, local bond measures; Congress for federal education law.
- **Healthcare and medical costs**: Congress, governor, state legislature, county health services, attorney general (enforcement).
- **Immigration**: Congress, president, governor and attorney general (state policy and litigation), sheriff (cooperation with federal enforcement), state legislature.
- **Climate, environment, and water**: Congress, governor, state legislature, utility commissions, county board and city council (land use), water districts.
- **Housing affordability**: City council, county board, state legislature, governor, mayor.
- **Elections and democracy**: Congress, state legislature, secretary of state, attorney general, county clerk or election officials, courts.
- **Crime and public safety**: Sheriff, district attorney, mayor, city council, state legislature, governor, attorney general.
- **Infrastructure**: Congress, governor, state legislature, county board, city council, special districts.
- **Technology and AI policy**: Congress, state legislature, attorney general (enforcement), governor.
- **Energy and utility costs**: Congress, governor, state legislature, utility or public service commission, city council and county board (local utilities and permitting).
- **Fiscal responsibility**: Every budget-setting office; treasurer, comptroller, auditor for oversight.
- **Veterans and military families**: Congress, governor, state veterans' agencies, county veteran services.
- **Gun policy**: Congress, state legislature, governor, attorney general, sheriff (enforcement and permitting).
- **Foreign policy and national security**: President, Congress, especially the Senate. Very limited at state and local levels.
- **Civil rights and equal treatment**: Congress, state legislature, attorney general, local officials via policies and enforcement.
- **Free speech and religious liberty**: Congress, state legislature, attorney general, school boards and local governments through their policies; courts decide disputes.
- **Trade and tariffs**: President and Congress mostly; governors and legislatures have limited roles (trade promotion, state incentives).
- **Small business and entrepreneurship**: Congress, governor, state legislature, mayor, city council, county board (licensing, permitting, taxes).
- **Local quality of life**: City council, mayor, county board, special districts.

When an issue spans levels (for example, immigration), tell the voter which part of it this office can touch ("this office sets county jail cooperation policy, not federal immigration law").
